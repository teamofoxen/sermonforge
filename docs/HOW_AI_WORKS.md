# How SermonForge Uses AI

A short guide so you know what you're working with.

---

## What AI is here for

In SermonForge, AI is a **second set of eyes** on work you've already done. It's not a sermon writer. It's not a research assistant you can hand the passage to and read back what comes out. Its job is small and specific: when you ask, it points at things you may have missed and asks you a question. Then it gets out of the way.

The phrase the system is built around is *clarity through constraint*. The system's job is to push you to do the clarity work — not to do it for you. Every place AI shows up in this app, that principle is doing the work. AI doesn't draft your sermon because the drafting is yours to do.

---

## What AI does

**Look Again.** At each Study sub-phase (Observe, Interpret, Redemptive Thread, Implications) there's a Look Again button. Once you've engaged at least one question, you can ask AI to read your work and surface 0–3 features of the passage you may not have engaged. Each one is a single plain-language question that sends you back to the text. Sometimes AI's response is just *"Your work covers what matters. Trust it and move on."* That's a real, valid answer — and it's the one you should hope for.

**MPS refinement.** When you're in MPT/MPS Forge, working a single sentence, you can talk back and forth with AI to refine it. AI translates from past-tense to present, checks for moralism drift, and helps tighten — all working on the words you've already written.

**Functional Elements chat.** When developing the Explanation, Application, and Illustration of an outline point, you can chat with AI about a specific point. Same pattern: AI helps develop what you've started, not generate from scratch.

**Manuscript audits.** Flow Coach, Ear Check, and Final Tune-Up read your manuscript and point at problems. Flow Coach checks whether each transition lands. Ear Check finds run-on sentences and orphaned ideas. Tune-Up gives an alignment audit. None of them write your prose for you — they tell you where to look.

**Delivery formatting.** When prepping for the pulpit, AI can format your manuscript for delivery (line breaks, breath marks) without changing a single word.

---

## What AI does not do

- It does not draft your Intro or Conclusion. Those are yours.
- It does not generate a sermon from a passage. There is no "give me a sermon on Romans 8" path.
- It does not pretend to be a biblical scholar. AI points at features in the text you can verify yourself; it doesn't issue verdicts on your hermeneutics.
- It does not write in your voice. Even where AI helps refine an MPS sentence, the goal is to clarify what you wrote — not to put words in your mouth.
- AI sometimes confidently states things that aren't in the text. The prompts are written to keep this rare — they ask AI to point at specific verifiable features rather than render scholarly verdicts — but you should always check what AI says against the actual passage. Trust your Bible over the assistant.

---

## What you'll notice

- **Open a sermon with no passage set.** The AI panel says *"Set a passage to begin. AI helps once you've started working the text."* No buttons. No chat. AI doesn't do anything before you've started.
- **Walk into a sub-phase with empty fields.** The Look Again button is disabled. Hover and you'll see *"Engage at least one question first."* Once you've genuinely engaged a question, the button enables.
- **No "Ask anything" chat box at most steps.** The free-form chat input only exists at MPT/MPS and Functional Elements, where multi-turn refinement is the actual work. Everywhere else you use Look Again or step-specific buttons.
- **AI's replies are short and bounded.** Look Again returns 0–3 questions, never a wall of scholarly critique. If AI thinks your work is sufficient, it says so and stops.

---

## Why it works this way

You're the preacher. The sermon is yours. The clarity work — sitting with the text, wrestling with what it says, deciding what to emphasize, finding your voice for this congregation this week — is yours.

AI's job is to be the friend who reads what you've written, asks one good question, and walks away so you can keep thinking.

The trade-off is real: AI is more constrained here than it is in tools that try to "help" by drafting. That constraint is the feature. The system asks questions you should be asking yourself; you answer them; AI helps you hear yourself more clearly.

That's it.
